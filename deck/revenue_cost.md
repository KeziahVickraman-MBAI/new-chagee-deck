# Revenue and cost structure: the four decision gates

Appendix A4 of the Chagee deck (OPIM639, Group 3). Generated by deck/build_deck.py from assumptions.json and the simulator output, so every figure matches the slide. Do not edit by hand. All figures are illustrative assumptions.

## The question

Does what the four gates (Predict, Provision, Counter, Review) save across Chagee's overseas openings cover the cost of building and running them? Over 5 years, at our approved assumptions, the modelled savings cover the cost of building and running the gates in year 5, leaving about S$289k ahead.

## How the analysis is structured

It follows the course examples of a revenue and cost analysis: a benefit line, one-time (fixed) costs, recurring (variable) costs, net benefit and cumulative net, year by year, with the assumption behind each line.

- **Benefit (the "revenue" side).** The modelled avoided cost from the benefit slide (slide 10): choosing each store's counter for its conditions instead of a mixed counter at every opening, plus a better first fresh-milk order. It is not sales revenue, and it covers no inventory other than fresh milk.
- **One-time costs.** Building the four gates and connecting the data (POS sales, app sign-ups, supplier orders). Year 1 only.
- **Recurring costs.** A maintenance team, a platform fee for every store using the gates, and one analyst day per opening for the Review gate.

## Where the benefit numbers come from

- **Counter saving, S$2,277 per store-year.** The simulator's yearly cost of the mixed counter above the cheapest of the three counters, averaged over openings spread evenly across our volume range. About S$455k a year for 200 stores. It recurs: a store keeps the cheaper counter.
- **Fresh-milk opening-stock saving, S$370 per opening.** The only inventory item quantified; tea, other ingredients and packaging have not been estimated. The best first milk order's expected cost with no pre-launch signal minus with the member signal. Once per opening. It holds only if member sign-ups narrow the demand range as assumed; that narrowing is our assumption, not a finding.
- **Store-years.** Stores opened in earlier years trade all year; stores opened this year trade half a year (openings spread through the year).

## Assumptions (approved 13 Oct 2026, illustrative)

## How to read it

- The payback is thin: it arrives in the last year of 5, and none of the three stress cases pays back within the horizon. The result depends on the counter saving, which grows only as stores accumulate.
- The benefit is a modelled avoided cost against a mixed counter at every opening. It is not a measured saving and not a demonstrated AI saving.
- No sales revenue is modelled: we have no price-per-cup or demand-elasticity assumption.
- The cost lines are our illustrative assumptions, not Chagee or vendor figures.
- The maintenance team is costed from year 1, alongside the build: conservative, and it may double-count part of year 1.

## If asked in Q&A (about 20 seconds)

"We set what the gates save against what they cost to build and run. At our assumptions they pay back in year 5, but only just, and not if build costs double, the counter saving halves, or openings halve. So the case rests on the per-store counter saving, which is what a pilot with real Chagee data should test first. The full table is in appendix A4."
