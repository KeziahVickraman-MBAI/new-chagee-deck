Our data and AI enablement initiatives have two parts, and they run on one system. Proposal one is dynamic demand management: it steers customers to capacity in the moment. Proposal two improves the bread and butter of the business, how the store is run: it plans staff, machines and stock ahead of time, while cutting overheads such as idle staff and wasted stock. This is AI-enabled in-store planning to achieve high-performing outlets. The two models are not separate tools: they share one data and AI system and inform each other. Routing decisions from proposal one feed the forecasts in proposal two, and the plans from proposal two tell proposal one where there is room. As more data builds up, every decision gets better. With AI, this can change: fewer lost sales, less idle staff, less wasted stock and a more optimal machinery count.

Click between Today and With AI on the chart. Today, a roster and a daily prep of fresh stock that do not flex day to day: on busy days demand rises above them, so queues form and stock runs out, which loses sales; in the school holiday demand falls below, so staff stand idle and unsold fresh milk and fruit is thrown away. This applies to perishables prepped daily, not tea leaves or packaging that keep. With AI, the plan tracks demand more closely. It will not match demand exactly, but the gaps get smaller: fewer lost sales, less idle staff and less wasted stock.

DBSCAN in four moves · toy example
  1. 1Radius ε. Two searches are neighbours if they are within ε of each other (the cluster-radius slider).
  2. 2Core. A search with at least the minimum number of searches within ε (counting itself) is a core point.
  3. 3Grow. A cluster spreads from core to core. Searches it reaches that are not core join as border points.
  4. 4Noise. Searches no cluster reaches. Unlike k-means, nobody picks the number of clusters, and odd shapes are fine.
