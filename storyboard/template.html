These two areas are connected: more customers generate more data, but they also create queues, while new outlets can take sales from existing ones. Our models examine those trade-offs. A lens from the course: on eBay, buyers and sellers attract each other: a cross-side network effect. Chagee is not that, in our reading: it sells its own tea, so there is no seller side to match. Customers to outlets is a pipeline sale.

What it does have sits on one side. One effect to grow: the data loop, like Netflix. More app users give more searches and orders, so the forecast and the site pick get sharper. This is why we chose Chagee: its business model is app-first and member-led. On its second-quarter 2026 earnings call, Chagee said it had {{facts.registered_members_jun2026|mn}} registered members at the end of June, and that members who made two or more purchases accounted for more than {{facts.repeat_member_order_share|pct}} of total orders. Those members sit on Chagee's mobile mini program, its ordering app, according to its first-quarter 2025 results. So nearly every cup leaves a data trail that can feed the loop. These are group-wide figures, not Singapore; Singapore's app share of orders is still to confirm. A same-side plus, buzz: queues and posts draw more drinkers; we do not model it.

And two crowding effects to cap. Congestion: at the peak, every extra customer lengthens everyone's wait; with a normal-day roster on an event day, about {{r.lost_normal_roster|pct}} of peak-hour orders walk away, and the forecast crew with a bucket brigade brings it to about {{r.lost_brigade|pct1}}. Cannibalisation: a new outlet takes cups from its neighbours; of {{r.top_cups}} cups at the app-search site, {{r.top_moved}} move from nearby outlets, against far more beside an existing outlet.

Cross-side effects exist, but on others' platforms: delivery apps and malls. Not our lever. About forty-five seconds.

      DBSCAN finds where searches bunch up; a proximity model turns a pin into cups

      DBSCAN in four moves · toy example
      1. 1Radius ε. Two searches are neighbours if they are within ε of each other (the cluster-radius slider).
      2. 2Core. A search with at least the minimum number of searches within ε (counting itself) is a core point.
      3. 3Grow. A cluster spreads from core to core. Searches it reaches that are not core join as border points.
      4. 4Noise. Searches no cluster reaches. Unlike k-means, nobody picks the number of clusters, and odd shapes are fine.
